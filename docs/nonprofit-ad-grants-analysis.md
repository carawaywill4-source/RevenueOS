# Google Ad Grants for a TributeReady grief-support affiliate

**Verdict: do not recommend** for a solo operator whose goal is attributable gross profit for TributeReady’s for-profit memorial products. A sibling 501(c)(3) can be legitimate for *charitable* grief education, but using Ad Grants primarily as a free acquisition channel for a related for-profit is blocked by IRS private-benefit rules and Google Ad Grants website / commercial-activity policies. Formation cost, delay, and compliance risk exceed expected net value versus free listing surfaces already wired into RevenueOS.

## Why Ad Grants looks attractive

Google for Nonprofits / Ad Grants can provide up to **$10,000/month** in in-kind Google Ads credit for eligible 501(c)(3) organizations (daily caps and keyword bid caps apply). See [Google Ad Grants overview](https://www.google.com/grants/) and [eligibility](https://support.google.com/grants/answer/9225410).

Requirements typically include: US 501(c)(3) status (or equivalent), validation (e.g. TechSoup), Google for Nonprofits enrollment, a qualifying nonprofit website, and ongoing program policies (quality / CTR / account activity).

## Why it fails as a TributeReady “free ads” path

1. **Private benefit / inurement.** A nonprofit may not be organized primarily to benefit a private for-profit (IRS 501(c)(3) requirements; see [IRS exemption requirements](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/charitable-organizations/exemption-requirements-501c3-organizations)). Steering Ad Grants traffic to TributeReady checkout as the core design is private benefit.

2. **Ad Grants commercial limits.** Ad Grants sites cannot operate as commercial storefronts for related businesses; Google prohibits misuse of the grant for commercial advertising of non-qualifying activity ([Ad Grants policies](https://support.google.com/grants/answer/6117705)).

3. **Formation cost / time for a solo operator.** Form 1023-EZ is cheaper/faster when eligible (~$275 filing fee historically; confirm current IRS fees), but full Form 1023 is often required for larger or more complex purposes and can take months. State incorporation, bylaws, board, annual filings, and TechSoup validation add ongoing overhead.

4. **Fiscal sponsorship** can accelerate operations under an existing 501(c)(3) umbrella, but **does not reliably unlock Ad Grants** for the sponsored project as a first-class grantee account — Ad Grants eligibility is tied to the qualifying nonprofit entity and Google for Nonprofits verification.

## If you still want a real nonprofit later

Only proceed if the charitable mission is primary (free grief education, bereavement resources, community support) with **incidental**, clearly disclosed, non-preferential mentions of commercial options — not a funnel. Budget ~$600–$2,000 year-one cash + 3–9 months before any Ad Grants account is live, then continuous policy compliance.

## Realistic $0 alternatives (recommended)

| Surface | Why it fits |
| --- | --- |
| Google Business Profile posts | Free local/search placements; no ad spend |
| Bing Places / Apple Business Connect | Free maps/local listings |
| Nextdoor Business posts | Free neighborhood newsfeed reach |
| Yelp free owner posts | Free local discovery |
| YouTube Shorts | Free algorithmic distribution |
| Organic SEO + GSC | Already in RevenueOS channel registry |

These do **not** require spend-match “free ad credits” (Google/Reddit/Microsoft new-advertiser offers that bill first, then credit).
